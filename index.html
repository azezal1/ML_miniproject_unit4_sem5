Theory

The three HMM problems

A Markov model assumes the next state depends only on the current one. A Hidden Markov Model adds a twist: you never see the state, only an observation it produces.

Evaluation · Forward

How likely is this scorecard?

Compute P(O | λ) by summing over every possible form path.

αt(j) = [ Σi αt−1(i) aij ] bj(ot)

Brute force checks NT paths. The forward algorithm reuses partial sums and needs only O(N²T).

Decoding · Viterbi

What was the form?

Find the single most likely hidden sequence.

δt(j) = maxi [ δt−1(i) aij ] bj(ot)

Same as forward, with the sum swapped for a max, plus back-pointers to recover the path.

Learning · Baum–Welch

What are the parameters?

Choose A, B, π to maximise the likelihood of the data.

aij = Σt ξt(i,j) / Σt γt(i)

EM: the E-step finds γ and ξ with forward–backward, the M-step re-estimates the parameters from them.

Viva questions

What is the Markov property?

The next state depends only on the current state, not on the full history. Here, today's form depends only on the form in the previous innings.

Why is the model called "hidden"?

We never observe the state (the batter's form). We only observe what it produces (the runs scored). The model infers the hidden state from the observations.

What are the parts of an HMM?

Hidden states, observations, the start probabilities π, the transition matrix A (state to state), and the emission matrix B (state to observation). Together λ = (A, B, π).

Why not just call a score of 50+ "in form"?

One innings is noisy. A good batter can fail once while still in form. The HMM uses the transition probabilities to look at the pattern over several innings, so one bad score doesn't flip the verdict.

Forward algorithm vs Viterbi?

Both fill the same table. Forward sums over previous states and gives the probability of the whole sequence. Viterbi takes the max and keeps back-pointers, which gives the single best hidden path.

What is the time complexity?

O(N²T) for N states and T innings, compared with O(NT) for checking every path by brute force.

Why do we need scaling factors?

Multiplying many probabilities below 1 quickly underflows to 0 on a computer. Scaling normalises α at each step by ct, and the log-likelihood becomes the sum of log ct. Viterbi uses log probabilities for the same reason.

How is Baum–Welch related to EM?

Baum–Welch is EM for HMMs. The E-step computes γ (state probabilities) and ξ (transition probabilities) with forward–backward. The M-step re-estimates A, B and π from them. Like K-means and GMMs in Unit 3, it can get stuck in a local maximum.

What is the sum-product algorithm here?

An HMM is a chain-shaped graphical model. Running sum-product message passing on that chain gives exactly the forward and backward recursions. Replacing sums with maxes gives Viterbi (max-product).

What is a linear dynamical system?

The continuous version of an HMM. The hidden state is a real-valued vector with Gaussian noise, and inference uses the Kalman filter and smoother instead of forward–backward.

Limitations of this project?

Runs are reduced to three buckets, so detail is lost. Real form also depends on the opponent, pitch and batting position. Learning the parameters from a short sequence overfits, so a longer career record gives a more reliable model.
